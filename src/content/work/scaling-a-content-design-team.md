When I stepped into the role in 2023, the content design team at Scotiabank was talented but stuck — reactive work, invisible pipeline, messy handoffs with product and design. Great craft was happening. Almost no one outside the team could see it. This is the story of how we changed that.

## The problem

The symptoms were familiar to anyone who has run a content function inside a large organization:

- Work arrived late, often as last-minute asks with no room to push back
- Stakeholders didn't know what we were working on or how to engage us
- Handoffs with product and design were inconsistent
- Senior designers were overloaded; junior designers had no clear growth path
- The team's impact was hard to articulate beyond individual launches

We needed stronger systems, clearer expectations, and a culture that empowered people to lead — not just execute.

## The approach

I treated this as two parallel problems: an operations problem and a culture problem. Fixing one without the other would have produced a team that ran on time but burned out, or a team that loved each other but missed deadlines. Both had to move together.

## What I did

**Built the team with intent.** I hired and onboarded six content designers with complementary skill sets — not six versions of the same person. The goal was a bench that could cover everything from conversational UI to dense regulated flows.

**Made the work visible.** I introduced a Workfront ticketing system so intake, capacity, and status were no longer tribal knowledge. Last-minute asks dropped because partners could see what was already in the queue.

**Created a path for people to grow.** I built and led a mentorship program that paired senior and junior designers across product teams, so growth didn't depend on who happened to sit next to whom.

**Installed team rituals.** Retros, documentation sprints, and monthly Community of Practice sessions kept the team aligned and made reuse a habit instead of an aspiration.

**Piloted AI tooling for component-based UI copy.** Early tests showed meaningful time savings on repetitive component work, freeing senior designers for higher-judgment problems.

**Handled the hard people call.** I facilitated a difficult but successful transition for one designer into a better-fit role inside the bank — the kind of decision that's easy to defer and expensive to delay.

**Tied operations to launches.** The intake system wasn't theoretical. It helped accelerate two platform rollouts: Global Transaction Banking and Investor Solutions.

## The results

- The team became the **go-to reviewers** for major content work across the org — a shift from "who do we send this to?" to "we already looped them in."
- Average **intake-to-delivery timelines dropped** as internal workflows tightened.
- Designers started **leading initiatives** beyond their own product squads — CoP sessions, AI prototypes, cross-team documentation.
- 360° feedback described the team as **"collaborative," "safe," and "high performing."**
- New hires ramped faster against better onboarding docs and clearer processes.

> The team became the group product partners wanted in the room — not the group they remembered to call at the end.

## Why this work matters

Building a strong content team goes beyond hiring great people. It's about creating the conditions for autonomy, trust, and momentum — the unglamorous scaffolding that lets craft show up consistently instead of heroically.

We didn't just grow headcount. We grew capability, credibility, and output. That's the difference between a team that ships and a team that's trusted to decide what should ship in the first place.
